# Grading standard for the four human-graded items

How to grade `evals/rubric.md`'s four human-graded items the way the candidate
does. The profile is the only truth about the candidate and the ad the only
truth about the employer.

## How to grade

1. **Per job, before reading any letter**, list the ad's must-haves (rules below)
   and mark each one **backed** (a profile entry describes doing it), **partly
   backed** (related evidence: the same activity with a different tool, or the
   same tool in a lighter role), or **not backed** (nothing, or only a bare skill
   listing). Write the list down.
2. Grade every letter for that job against that same list.
3. Grade each letter on its own against this standard, never relative to the
   other letter for the same job. Note the reason for every N.

**What is a must-have.** The core, distinguishing requirements: the ad's
essentials / requirements / "you'll bring" list, plus the role's defining
activity. A heading like "preferably have" over the whole list does not demote
it. Never must-haves: eligibility and admin (work rights, citizenship,
clearances, licences, checks, availability), attitudes and traits, benefits,
items marked ideally / familiarity / nice to have / highly regarded / bonus,
grad-level "contribute to X" duties, and items implied by a stronger one (git
under "builds production web apps"). A listed duty is not a must-have unless the
ad asks for prior experience in it. Expect 3-6; grouping them is fine.

## supported_musts_covered

- **Y**: every backed or partly backed must-have is addressed with the
  evidence the profile holds for it.
- **N**: any backed or partly backed must-have is missing, addressed only with
  weaker evidence when the profile holds stronger, or denied.

A partly backed must-have counts as addressed when framed honestly as related
experience ("I used tool A, which carries over to tool B"); omitting it fails.
Not-backed must-haves may be omitted.

Borderline rulings:
- Covering a technical must-have (stakeholder or user communication, a framework)
  with a non-technical job when the profile has direct technical evidence: N.
- "I am new to X" or "I have not used X" when the profile backs X: N (and it
  is not addressed).
- "I have not done X, but..." for a genuinely not-backed must-have: neutral.
  It neither covers nor fails this item.
- Leaning on one strong project and leaving the professional experience vague
  ("valuable exposure") so its backed must-haves never appear: N.

Strictness: high (see Calibration notes).

## no_unsupported_claims

- **Y**: every factual claim about the candidate traces to a profile entry, and
  every claim about the employer traces to the ad.
- **N**: one or more claims do not. A single unsupported claim is N.

Fine: paraphrase that keeps the same activity, scope and audience;
self-assessment adjectives ("strong", "solid") on a listed skill; naming a
listed core language as used on a described project where that is plausible;
understating (understatement can still hurt would_send).

A stretch (N):
- a different audience, seniority or role ("executives" for team leads, "led"
  for contributed);
- rounded-up numbers, or outcomes, tools or audiences that are not in the profile;
- "practical / hands-on experience in X", "apply my skills in X" or "exposure
  to X" resting on a bare listing or a job title alone;
- building X presented as using X day to day; the same subject with a
  different activity;
- invented process claims ("through rigorous testing", "ensuring data
  integrity") attached to a project whose entry does not say so;
- employer facts (products, values, programs) not in the ad.

Tone or fact? If an interviewer asked "tell me about the time you did that"
and the profile has no answer, it is a fact claim and it is N.

## specific_detail

- **Y**: at least one fact from the ad about this employer, product, team,
  project, program or platform is used meaningfully: tied to why the candidate
  wants the role or how their experience applies.
- **N**: only the role title, the company name, contract length, generic
  industry phrases ("social impact", "diverse projects", "innovative
  solutions"), or a restated requirement.

Test: swap in another employer advertising the same title. If the sentence
still fits after changing only the name, it does not count.

Strictness: moderate (see Calibration notes).

## would_send

- **Y**: the candidate would send it after light edits.
- **N**: it needs more than light edits.

**Light edits:** word-level fixes (spelling, a salutation, a swapped word)
plus changing, deleting or adding at most two sentences in total. Any
restructuring, a new or reordered paragraph, or replacing the main example is
more than light.

Not sendable as-is (any one of these makes it N):
- supported_musts_covered or specific_detail is N, or fixing an unsupported
  claim takes more than the light-edit budget;
- over 340 words;
- a sentence that leads with a gap ("While I am new to...", "Although my
  background is not in..."). One short clause of honest related-experience
  framing is fine;
- no clear reason for wanting this role or employer, or experience listed
  without saying how it applies to the work in the ad ("more passion, reason
  or application");
- stock phrasing ("I am writing to express my interest") or generic filler in
  more than one place, or the same awkward phrase or achievement repeated;
- a machine-written feel: three or more tells, e.g. "demonstrating my ability
  to" tails, "translate seamlessly", triple adjectives, stacked intensifiers;
- more than three US spellings (Australian spelling is the chosen convention).

For a Y, all must hold: every backed must-have shown with its best evidence;
the strongest professional experience in concrete detail; an employer-specific
reason for this role; each paragraph applying evidence to the ad's work; reads
like a person, 250-340 words.

Strictness: very high. "Could send" is not Y; Y means the candidate would be
happy to send it.

## Calibration notes

- **supported_musts_covered: strict.** 2 Y, 3 N. Every N had relevant
  work-placement evidence (the right framework, version control, AI tooling,
  stakeholder engagement) and used an academic project and a non-technical job
  instead, or denied a backed skill. Omitting a not-backed must-have was never
  penalised.
- **no_unsupported_claims: lenient on tone, strict on facts.** The candidate
  passed 5 of 5, tolerating confident adjectives and plausible tool attribution.
  The binding rulings add hard fails for stretched scope, audience, numbers and
  bare-listing experience claims. Apply those rulings even where the
  calibration grades look forgiving.
- **specific_detail: moderate.** 3 Y, 2 N. Passes named a specific team,
  program, platform or distinctive duty and connected it to the candidate.
  Fails put the company name on generic praise or echoed a listed requirement.
- **would_send: very strict.** 0 of 9 one-shot letters, one noted "could send
  but it could use more passion, reason or application". What the candidate
  values most is passion, a reason for this role, and applying the evidence to
  the job. Every rejected letter had stock phrasing or an underused
  professional placement; three also had gap-led sentences.
- **Ambiguity resolved:** gap sentences are neutral for coverage but fail
  would_send unless phrased as related experience.
